Overview

A crime that attacks feelings, not intelligence

Scams are one of the largest industries in the world. They reach every country, every age group, every level of education. Doctors, lawyers, bank managers and police officers are all being scammed. The reason è is simple — and è is the most important idea of ​​this relationship.

The thesis

Scammers don't attack your intelligence

They attack your feelings. A scam è a story told to make you act before you think. The defense doesn't è be smarter — è still build time to think.

Part I · Understanding scams

The anatomy of a scam

Almost every scam in the world tries to get one of three things from you. You understand this, and you understand the whole machine.

What the scammer is really looking for

Objective 01

Your money

Send money, transfer money, pay a fee, buy gift cards or hand over your bank details for them to take themselves. The most obvious objective 3 and the payment method 4 is always their choice, never yours.

Objective 02

Your information

Sometimes they don't ask for money at all. They want your password, card number, PIN, a code sent to your phone, a copy of your passport or your date of birth — to steal later, open accounts in your name or take over your accounts.

Objective 03

Your help

What surprises people. They want you to receive money, forward a package, buy with your card or open an account. You think you're helping or making money. In reality you are a shield 1 and now the crime 2 is partly in your name.

The ten psychological buttons

Button 01

Hope and greedà

Something for nothing — always «barely credible». Not one billion euros, but fifty thousand. Big enough to change your life, small enough to seem possible.

Button 02

Fear

«Your account è has been hacked.» «C’è a warrant for your arrest.» Fear shuts down careful thinking, and the scammer offers the quickest way to stop it.

Button 03

Love and loneliness

The most powerful button of all, and the cruelest. The victim often defends the scammer, because believing the truth means losing the only person who seems to care about her.

Button 04

Authority

Police, banks, tax authorities: we obey. They use official logos, official language, spoofed caller ID, and near-perfect email addresses. When the authority speaks, we stop doubting.

Button 05

Urgency

«Within 24 hours.» Speedà è is the 4fraudster's ally and the victim's enemy. A deadline is fabricated to keep you from checking.

Button 06

Shame

They are counting on your silence. If you are too embarrassed to tell anyone, no one can warn you and the scam continues unabated.

Button 07

The foot in the door

A small first sì — a small favor, a small payment — makes the next, larger sù4 coherent and natural.

Button 08

The sunk cost trap

You've already paid, so paying once again will finally unlock 233 wins. This is why victims pay again and again.

Button 09

Social proof

A chat group full of «happy» customers, all fake. If everyone does it, it must be safe 3 too bad that 4 all 5 is also the scammer.

Button 10

Illusion of control

«You can withdraw whenever you want.» The panel, the numbers, the VIP levels — all designed to make you feel in control of your money.

The scammer's script — seven steps, every time

1 · The 1st approach, or the 2nd one
Unexpected contact — email, SMS, call, friend request, a «wrong number»
2 · Building trust, or courtship
Warmth, a sad story, «evidence» of earnings, a chat group of fake happy customers
3 · Creating the need
An urgent and emotional reason — and the solution è as long as you send money
4 · The first payment
A small sum, or a piece of information designed to appear harmless
5 · L’escalation
Bigger sums, more pressure, a new deadline every time
6 · L’extraction
Everything — savings, loans, even other people's money
7 · Disappearance, or ghosting
Silence, or a new identity and a new target

The scam economy — where the money goes

Stage 01

Your payment

A payment goes out to you. Its type decides how difficult it will be to get it back 1 and the scammer always chooses the most difficult to reverse.

Stage 02

The figurehead

A «money mule» gets it — often recruited with a fake job: «get paid, keep five percent».

Stage 03

The layers

It is moved, usually broken down into smaller sums, across accounts in different countries.

Stage 04

L’collection

Converted to crypto or cash and gone within hours. This is why speed1 matters so much.

Who is behind

The’industry

The «compounds of scams» in Southeast Asia

The largest organized operations are not in Africa. In Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos, gated compounds house hundreds or thousands of workers who run pig butchering, fake investments and romance scams in shifts, with managers, targets and human resources departments. Many of the workers are themselves victims — lured by fake jobs, with their passports confiscated, forced to work under threat of violence.

The subculture

The «Yahoo Boys» and the culture of fraud

In some parts of West Africa, computer fraud has become a subculture that treats fraud as a game and a status symbol. Luxury photographs bought with stolen money, its own slang, music and heroes. It harms everyone, including honest citizens and businesses in the countries where it exists.

«Don't imagine a lone criminal. You might be talking to a person who is themselves a prisoner, reading a script written by someone else, in a building on the other side of the world.»

Case study · L’«trading academy» that è vanished

Chapter 14.4

A couple, a Telegram group and €150,000

An Italian couple in their fifties signed up to a trading academy on Telegram recommended by a friend who had already earned 5 6 6 himself a victim, paid with a small commission to recruit others. They started with €1,000, saw daily profits on the panel and in eight months had invested almost €150,000, including a bank loan and the sale of a’car. When they asked to withdraw, the platform demanded a «unlocking fee of 22%». They paid part of it. Then the site, the group and every administrator disappeared.

Chapter 12.4

An accountant, a «financial analyst» and €200,000

A 40-year-old accountant from Lombardy received a WhatsApp message from a woman who introduced herself as a financial analyst. After three months of daily conversations, he had transferred almost €200,000 to a platform showing a balance of over €300,000 — and told his brother about the «best investment of his life». The withdrawal was refused unless he paid a «tax» of 20%. He borrowed €40,000 to pay for it. Then the site went offline. The funds had crossed several countries to crypto within hours of each transfer. He's still paying off the loan.

Parts V, VI, VII · Recovery

If you have already been scammed

Speed ​​matters more than anything else. In the first hours the money can still stop; after a few days, usually not. Do these things in order — and do the thing that first not you have to do: never let shame keep you silent.

The first 24 hours — act in this order

Hour 01

Call your bank

Ask to stop or recall the payment. Request the recall in writing and write down the reference. Don't wait for the morning.

Hour 01

Block and freeze

Cancel cards, freeze accounts, change passwords and activate 2FA — from your email, the master key to everything.

First day

Collect the evidence

Screenshots, messages, scammer's IBAN, emails with full headers. Do not erase anything or do «cleaning».

First day

Report to the police

File a complaint or complaint (Chapter 73). The complaint is what opens the doors that an individual cannot open alone.

The recovery scam — the 1attack that comes second

Anyone who offers to get your money back for an upfront fee is again a thief. Victims are the easiest targets, because they are the most desperate. The warning signs are unmistakable.

Signal 01

Contacts you first

The real recovery work doesn't start with an outgoing call. If someone found you, ask how — and why é right now.

Signal 02

Promises recovery

No one can promise recovery of stolen funds. Not a lawyer, not a firm, not an IT specialist.

Signal 03

Wants a commission upfront

True professionals charge for their time, or take a percentage of what is recovered - never an advance payment.

Signal 04

Asks for crypto or gift cards

No legitimate service requires payment in this way. Never.

Signal 05

Perfect and anonymous reviews

Real reviews cite difficulties, delays, and failures. A wall of spotless five-star posts è written by its owner.

Signal 06

He wants documents and urgency

It asks for your bank details, a document and keys — and a decision before you can check out. Both are the mark of fraud.

The ten rules to remember forever

Rule 01

Slow down

Every scam depends on speed. Time is your strongest defense.

Rule 02

Never pay money to receive money

Any upfront fee — for a prize, a’inheritanceà, a loan or a job — è a scam.

Rule 03

Never give a code, PIN or password

No bank, police officer or technician needs it. The code on your phone is è just for you.

Rule 04

There is no such thing as a «secure account»

Anyone who asks you to move your money to protect it is stealing it.

Rule 05

Check via a channel of your choice

Hang up and call back on a number you found yourself. Never trust the number or link in the message.

Rule 06

Trust the process, not the impression

Judge with controls, not with feelings. A person who resists being controlled is telling you something.

Rule 07

If it's too good to be true, don't è

A guaranteed profit, a prize with a commission, a stranger who loves you in a week: none of this is real.

Rule 08

Preserve the evidence and act quickly

In the early hours, call your bank. Don't delete anything. Report to the police.

Rule 09

Don't be ashamed and don't be silent

Talk to someone about it. Shame protects the deceiver; the opening defeats him.

Rule 10

Watch out for the second scam

Anyone who offers to get your money back for an upfront fee is a thief, again.
